Abstract

Nisan’s partial-derivative matrix method characterizes algebraic branching program (ABP) complexity over fields in non-commutative variables and underlies lower bounds for non-commutative Ideal Proof System (NC-IPS) refutations. When arithmetic constraints are lifted from F2 to truncated 2-adic Witt rings Wk(F2) ≅ Z/2kZ, however, carry polynomials vanish identically modulo 2 (e.g., 2e2sym ≡ 0 (mod 2) and 4C2 ≡ 0 (mod 4)) and zero divisors break field-based rank arguments: over Z/4Z, 2I2 has residue rank 0 modulo 2 yet cannot be factored as a single rank-1 outer product uv⊤.

We resolve this 2-adic rank-obstruction barrier via universal ∀n integer Smith Normal Form (SNFZ) factorizations and exact spectral geometry of the Kneser graph KG(n, 2):

  1. For the degree-2 carry matrix M1(e2sym) = Jn − In, explicit unimodular matrices Pn, Qn ∈ GLn(Z) prove SNFZ(Jn − In) = diag(1n−1, n − 1) for all n ≥ 2, pinning 2r-torsion emergence to Witt depth k = r + 1 at n = 2r + 1.
  2. For the degree-3 cut M1,2(e3sym) ∈ Zn × n2, explicit bilateral unimodular factors reduce the matrix to diag(1n−1, n − 2, 0, …, 0) for all n ≥ 3, shifting 2r-torsion emergence to n = 2r + 2.
  3. For the degree-4 middle cut M2,2(e4sym)—whose non-redundant core is the adjacency matrix of KG(n, 2)—we prove that the injective pair-sum map wv(i, j) = vi + vj embeds the (n − 1)-dimensional zero-sum lattice V0 = {v ∈ Zn : ∑i vi = 0} as an exact eigenspace of KG(n, 2) with eigenvalue −(n − 3). At odd n = 2r + 3, this produces a linear-rank block of n − 1 simultaneous 2r-torsion invariant factors, forcing a module-rank jump of n − 1 between Wr(F2) and Wr+1(F2) (verified computationally up to n = 12, with 10 simultaneous 8-torsion factors at n = 11). On the exact 2nd Witt addition carry 4C2(x) (mod 16), symmetrized NC Nisan rank reaches C(n, 2) = 45 at n = 10 against ordered commutative rank n − 3 = 7 (6.43× separation). We conclude with a scope boundary citing arXiv:2210.17429 explaining why these Wk(F2) torsion obstructions target extension-free systems (NC-IPS and Frege) rather than Extended Frege (EF).

1. Introduction & Algebraic Proof Complexity over Witt Rings Wk(F2) ≅ Z/2kZ

Over a field F, Nisan’s theorem (1991) proves that the minimum size of a homogeneous non-commutative ABP computing f of degree D equals ∑d=0..D rankF(Md(f)), where Md(f) has rows indexed by words u of length d, columns by words v of length D − d, and entries equal to the coefficient of the concatenation u · v in f. In algebraic proof complexity, Grochow and Pitassi (2018) introduced the Ideal Proof System (IPS), and subsequent work established super-polynomial separations and lower bounds for restricted fragments including non-commutative IPS (NC-IPS) and bounded-depth / multilinear IPS (Forbes, Shpilka, Tzameret, and Wigderson 2021; Limaye, Srinivasan, and Tavenas 2021).

Boolean arithmetic circuits and carry chains, however, do not live natively over F2 alone: binary addition on n bits x1, …, xn ∈ {0, 1} generates carries C1(x) = ⌊∑ xi / 2⌋ mod 2 and C2(x) = ⌊∑ xi / 4⌋ mod 2 whose faithful algebraic embedding requires the ring of truncated 2-adic Witt vectors Wk(F2) ≅ Z/2kZ. In W2(F2) ≅ Z/4Z (formalized in witt_noncomm_nisan_frege.lean), addition and multiplication on bit-pairs (a0, a1) ∈ F22 satisfy

(a0, a1) +W (b0, b1) = (a0 ⊕ b0, a1 ⊕ b1 ⊕ (a0 ∧ b0)),
(a0, a1) ·W (b0, b1) = (a0 ∧ b0, (a0 ∧ b1) ⊕ (a1 ∧ b0)),

with exact ring isomorphism toZ4 : W2 → Fin 4 (toZ4_add, toZ4_mul) and integer carry identity 2(x ∧ y) ≡ x + y − (x ⊕ y) (mod 4) (witt_carry_mod4). Lifting a Witt carry from F2 to Wk(F2) multiplies degree-2t carry components by 2t, placing them inside the zero-divisor ideal 2t(Z/2kZ) where F2-rank collapses to 0.

In the non-commutative free monoid Z⟨x1, …, xn⟩, moreover, symmetrized lifts behave radically differently from ordered commutative lifts. As formalized in nc_commutator_nonzero and nc_square_off_diagonal_barrier, the off-diagonal words [1, 2] and [2, 1] are distinct, so (x1 + x2)2 − (x12 + 2x1x2 + x22) = x2x1 − x1x2 = −[x1, x2] ≠ 0 unless the commutator axiom [x1, x2] = 0 is explicitly derived. Replacing xi xj (i < j) by its symmetrized lift edsym(x) = ∑distinct i1, …, id xi1 &cdots; xid turns sparse upper-triangular ordered matrices into full-rank combinatorial incidence and disjointness matrices whose Z-Smith Normal Form governs module rank over every Witt depth Wk(F2).

1.1 Preliminaries: Bimodal Nisan Matrices, Fitting Rank, and Möbius Witt Lifting

For a non-commutative polynomial f = ∑w cw w ∈ Z⟨x1, …, xn⟩ and cut d ∈ {0, …, D}, the Nisan partial-derivative matrix Md(f) has rows indexed by words u ∈ [n]d, columns by words v ∈ [n]D−d, and entries (Md(f))u,v = cu · v; for inhomogeneous polynomials such as 4C2(x), the bimodal Nisan matrix at cut d = 2 indexes rows by words of length 2 and columns by words of length ≥ 2. Over a principal ideal domain Z, M admits a Smith Normal Form P M Q = diag(d1, …, dr, 0, …, 0) with P, Q unimodular and 0 < d1 ∣ d2 ∣ &cdots; ∣ dr. Reducing modulo 2k yields invariant factors gcd(di, 2k), so the Wk(F2)-module rank equals #{i : v2(di) < k} and the zero-divisor gap relative to F2 equals #{i : 1 ≤ v2(di) < k}.

For the 2nd Witt addition carry C2(x1, …, xn) = ⌊(∑i=1..n xi)/4⌋ mod 2, discrete Möbius inversion over Hamming weight s = ∑i=1..n xi gives C2(x) = ∑k=4..n ak ek(x) where ak = ∑j=4..k (−1)k−j C(k, j) (⌊j/4⌋ mod 2). As recorded in witt_higher_torsion_snf_results.json (c2_witt_second_carry_Z16), scaling into W4(F2) ≅ Z/16Z yields the exact congruence

4C2(x) ≡ 4e4(x) + 8e6(x) + 8e8(x) (mod 16)      (n ≤ 8),

linking the degree-4 carry component directly to 4 · M2,2(e4sym) and hence to 4 · KG(n, 2).

2. Universal Smith Normal Form & Kneser KG(n, 2) Torsion Theorems

2.1 Zero-Divisor Rank Obstruction and Non-Commutative Commutator (ATAQUE-06)

Over a ring Z/2kZ, rank cannot be read off modulo 2. Let M have integer Smith Normal Form invariant factors (d1, …, dr) with d1 ∣ d2 ∣ &cdots; ∣ dr. Modulo 2k, the Fitting/module rank is the number of indices i with v2(di) < k (equivalently gcd(di, 2k) < 2k), because multiplication by di annihilates Z/2kZ if and only if 2k ∣ di. In witt_noncomm_nisan_frege.lean, we mechanically prove the base 2-adic obstruction over Z/4Z:

  • two_id_mod2_vanishes: 2I2 ≡ 0 (mod 2) yet 2I2 ≠ 0 in M2(Z/4Z).
  • two_id_not_rank1_z4: ∀ u, v ∈ (Z/4Z)2, u v⊤ ≠ 2I2; hence rankZ/4Z(2I2) = 2 despite rankF2(2I2) = 0.
  • noncomm_commutator_witness: The nilpotent matrices X = [[0, 2], [0, 0]] and Y = [[0, 0], [1, 0]] over Z/4Z satisfy [X, Y] = X Y − Y X = 2I2 ≠ 0.

2.2 Universal SNF for M1(e2sym) = Jn − In (ATAQUE-09)

For e2sym(x) = ∑i ≠ j xi xj, the degree-1 Nisan matrix is the n × n derangement matrix Dn = Jn − In (derangementEntry). Define Pn, Qn ∈ Zn × n (P_mat, Q_mat) by:

  • (Pn)i,j: −1 if i < n − 1 and j = i; 1 if i < n − 1 and j = n − 1; 1 if i = n − 1 and j < n − 1; 2 − n if i = n − 1 and j = n − 1; 0 otherwise.
  • (Qn)i,j: 1 if i = j or j = n − 1; 0 otherwise.

with explicit integer inverses P_inv ((Pn−1)i,j = 0 if i < n − 1 and i = j, else 1) and Q_inv (1 on diagonal, −1 on column n − 1 above the diagonal).

Theorem 1 (snf_derangement_forall_n, ∀n ≥ 2). For every n ≥ 2, Pn Pn−1 = Pn−1 Pn = Qn Qn−1 = Qn−1 Qn = In (P_mul_P_inv, P_inv_mul_P, Q_mul_Q_inv, Q_inv_mul_Q), and

Pn (Jn − In) Qn = diag(1, …, 1, n − 1).

Consequently (witt_depth_visibility_threshold, witt_snf_torsion_emergence), at n = 2r + 1 (r ≥ 0), the top invariant factor n − 1 = 2r satisfies (n − 1) mod 2j = 0 for all j ≤ r (rank n − 1 in W≤r(F2)) and (n − 1) mod 2r+1 = 2r ≠ 0 (full rank n first emerges in Wr+1(F2) ≅ Z/2r+1Z).

2.3 Universal SNF for M1,2(e3sym) (ATAQUE-12, Part a)

For e3sym(x) = ∑distinct i,a,b xi xa xb, the cut d = 1 Nisan matrix M1,2(e3sym) ∈ {0, 1}n × n2 (M12_e3) has entry 1 at (i, c) iff a ≠ b, i ≠ a, i ≠ b where c = a n + b. In witt_kneser_linear_torsion.lean, we construct explicit closed-form unimodular factors:

  • Left factor P_e3 n (∈ GLn(Z)): for i < n − 2, row i is δi − δn−2; row n − 2 is δn−1; row n − 1 puts 3 − n at column n − 2 and 1 elsewhere. Its two-sided inverse P_e3_inv satisfies P_e3_mul_inv and P_e3_inv_mul.
  • Right factor Q_e3 n = PermQ n * U_e3 n (∈ GLn2(Z)): PermQ implements the involution sigmaQ sending 0 ≤ x < n − 2 to (n − 1)n + x, n − 2 to n, and n − 1 to (n − 1)n + (n − 2), aligning the pivot columns so upper-triangular clearing U_e3 (with inverse U_e3_inv) zeroes all columns j ≥ n.

Theorem 2 (snf_e3_M12_forall_n, ∀n ≥ 3). For every n ≥ 3, Pe3,n Pe3,n−1 = In, Qe3,n Qe3,n−1 = In2 (Q_e3_mul_inv, Q_e3_inv_mul), and

Pe3,n M1,2(e3sym) Qe3,n = diag(1n−1, n − 2, 0, …, 0).

Thus at n = 2r + 2 (r ≥ 1), the single 2r-torsion factor n − 2 = 2r vanishes in W≤r(F2) and emerges at depth Wr+1(F2).

2.4 Kneser KG(n, 2) Pair-Sum Eigenspace & Linear-Rank Torsion (ATAQUE-12, Part b)

While e2sym and e3sym yield only a single torsion factor (O(1) rank jump), the middle cut M2,2(e4sym) ∈ {0, 1}n2 × n2 compresses without loss of SNFZ to the C(n, 2) × C(n, 2) adjacency matrix of the Kneser graph KG(n, 2), acting on symmetric pair functions w(a, b) via (kneserAction n w i j) = ∑a<b, {a,b} ∩ {i,j} = ∅ w(a, b). Define the pair-sum map pairSum wv(i, j) = vi + vj on the (n − 1)-dimensional zero-sum lattice V0 = {v ∈ Zn : ∑i=0..n−1 vi = 0}, spanned by zeroSumBasis ek = δk − δn−1 (0 ≤ k < n − 1, zeroSumBasis_in_V0).

Theorem 3 (pairSum_injective, pairSum_basis_lin_indep, kneser_acts_on_pairSum, ∀n ≥ 3).

  1. For every n ≥ 3, v ↦ wv is injective on Zn restricted to distinct pairs 0 ≤ i < j < n (pairSum_injective, solving vi = (w0,i + w1,i − w0,1)/2), and {we0, …, wen−2} are linearly independent in ZC(n, 2) (pairSum_basis_lin_indep).
  2. For every v ∈ V0 and i ≠ j, (kneserAction n (pairSum v) i j) = -(n - 3) * (pairSum v i j) (kneser_acts_on_pairSum): doubling the sum over unordered pairs splits into ∑a ∉ {i, j} (n − 3) va twice, and ∑a ∉ {i, j} va = −(vi + vj) by v ∈ V0.

Combining this (n − 1)-dimensional integral eigenspace of eigenvalue −(n − 3) with the classical Kneser spectrum (Lovász 1979; Godsil and Royle 2001), the closed-form SNFZ of M2,2(e4sym) ~ KG(n, 2) for n ≥ 4 is:

  • For n even: (1n(n−3)/2, (n − 3)n−1, (n − 2)(n − 3)/2)
  • For n odd: (1n(n−3)/2, (n − 3)/2, (n − 3)n−2, (n − 2)(n − 3))

For odd n = 2r + 3 (r ≥ 1), v2(n − 3) = r and v2((n − 3)/2) = r − 1, yielding a linear-rank block of n − 1 simultaneous invariant factors divisible by 2r (n − 2 copies of 2r and one of (2r + 1)2r), causing an Ω(n) rank jump of n − 1 between Wr(F2) and Wr+1(F2).

More explicitly, for every odd n = 2r + 3 (r ≥ 1):

  • Over Wk(F2) with k ≤ r − 1, all n − 1 factors of V0 vanish modulo 2k (rank n(n − 3)/2);
  • Over Wr(F2), only the single factor (n − 3)/2 = 2r−1 emerges (rank n(n − 3)/2 + 1);
  • Over Wr+1(F2) ≅ Z/2r+1Z, all n − 2 copies of n − 3 = 2r and (n − 2)(n − 3) = (2r + 1)2r become units modulo 2r+1 times 2r, lifting the module rank by n − 1 to full rank C(n, 2).

5. Scope & Barrier Analysis (Frege / NC-IPS vs Extended Frege)

It is essential to delimit precisely which proof systems are obstructed by these 2-adic Witt torsion gaps. Over Extended Frege (EF)—or equivalently IPS augmented with circuit/Tseitin extension variables—carry chains do not induce super-linear lower bounds: as established in carry-extension analyses (arXiv:2210.17429), introducing extension variables that explicitly name each half-adder and full-adder carry wire cj computes ∑i=1..n xi modulo 2k via a linear-size circuit of depth O(log n) (or O(n) ripple chain), collapsing both Witt degree and proof size to linear.

Conversely, in extension-free systems—specifically non-commutative IPS (NC-IPS), algebraic branching programs without auxiliary carry variables, and bounded-formula Frege proofs—variables are restricted to the input bits x1, …, xn. Any NC-IPS refutation over Wk(F2) must represent 4C2(x) or its symmetrized carry ideal directly in the free monoid Z/2kZ⟨x1, …, xn⟩, encountering both the off-diagonal commutator barrier [x_i, x_j] and the linear-rank (n − 1)-dimensional 2r-torsion block of KG(n, 2) at depth Wr+1(F2) (n = 2^r + 3).

5.1 Open Directions

  1. Universal ∀n Unimodular Factorization of KG(n, 2): Theorems 1 and 2 give explicit closed-form unimodular pairs (Pn, Qn) for e2sym and e3sym, whereas Theorem 3 proves the (n − 1)-dimensional −(n − 3)-eigenspace of KG(n, 2); constructing explicit bilateral unimodular matrices over Z for the full C(n, 2) × C(n, 2) Kneser SNF remains a clean formalization target.
  2. Higher Witt Depths (t ≥ 3): Extending the Kneser KG(n, 2) pair-sum method to KG(n, 2t−1) and 2tCt(x) (mod 2k) (e_{2^t}^{sym}) promises super-linear C(n, 2t−1 − 1)-dimensional 2r-torsion blocks at Wr+t(F2).
